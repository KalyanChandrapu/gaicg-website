About

Why Global AI Compliance Guild exists

AI is being adopted quickly across financial services, healthcare and other regulated sectors. Governance requirements are arriving from many directions at once, and implementation guidance is fragmented. Organisations are left to translate regulatory intent into technical controls on their own.

Global AI Compliance Guild exists to close that gap, slowly and carefully, with work that can be checked.

Independent

No single organisation's product or commercial interest shapes our output.

Sourced

Every regulatory statement cites its primary source and version date.

Versioned

Every publication carries a version number, an effective date and a change log.

Corrected in public

Charter

What we do, and how we work

Draft v0.1

Purpose

To help organisations understand and implement AI compliance requirements through sourced, practical and independently produced guidance.

Scope

  • Geographies: India, United Kingdom, European Union, United States, and the Middle East (first named jurisdictions to be confirmed).
  • Sectors: financial services first; healthcare to follow.
  • Reference frameworks: NIST AI Risk Management Framework and ISO/IEC 42001, treated as voluntary standards.

Workstreams

  • Regulatory register: a versioned record of AI obligations, their status, applicability and sources.
  • Control framework: controls mapped to the register, linking regulation, obligation, control, evidence and test.
  • Assurance model: test procedures, evidence expectations and a maturity scale.
  • Publications: one whitepaper per quarter, with shorter guidance notes in between.

Working model

  • The group meets fortnightly.
  • Decisions are made by consensus. Where consensus fails, the convenor decides and records the reasoning.
  • Roles rotate, and written processes cover publishing, review and correction.
  • Members participate in a personal capacity. Members must not discuss pricing, customers or competitive strategy of their employers.

Review

The charter is reviewed annually. If a quarter passes without a publication or register update, the group meets to decide whether to continue, change scope or pause.

Entry 1: Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (India)

InstrumentDigital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act No. 22 of 2023), and Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025
RegulatorData Protection Board of India
StatusEnacted. The Rules were notified in November 2025 and the Act's obligations take effect in phases over about 18 months. The final compliance date is around May 2027; sources differ by a day, so confirm against the notification.
ApplicabilityProcessing of digital personal data in India, and processing outside India connected with offering goods or services to people in India (S.3). Applies to Data Fiduciaries, Significant Data Fiduciaries, Data Processors and Consent Managers.
Primary sourceAct No. 22 of 2023, Gazette of India. Link to be added.
Last checked5 November 2026, against the Act text as recorded in the group's working register. Gazette check outstanding.

Entry 2: Model Risk Management guidance (RBI, draft 2026)

InstrumentGuidance on Regulatory Principles for Model Risk Management, 2026 (draft). Press Release 2026-2027/528, 24 June 2026. The circular number is not yet assigned in the draft.
RegulatorReserve Bank of India
StatusDraft for consultation The comment period closed on 24 July 2026. This entry does not confirm whether a final version has been issued. Check the RBI website before relying on it.
ApplicabilityParagraph 4 lists commercial banks (including foreign banks), small finance banks, payments banks, local area banks, regional rural banks, urban and rural co-operative banks, NBFCs (base, middle, upper and top layers), all-India financial institutions, asset reconstruction companies and credit information companies. Some press coverage describes a narrower scope. Paragraph 4 governs.
Effective dateNot stated in the draft
Relationship to other rulesRead with RBI Directions. Where they conflict, the Directions prevail (para 5). Applies to all models, whether built internally or bought from third parties (para 6). On final issue, supersedes Chapter 3 of the 2002 Guidance Note on Credit Risk Management (para 64).
Primary sourceRBI draft text, 16 pages. Link to the RBI press release to be added.
Last checked5 November 2026, against the draft text. The draft says further AI requirements may follow (para 2), so this entry will need a further review.

Key AI provisions (Chapter V.B) and related paragraphs

ParagraphRequirement
9A Board-approved MRMF must cover AI and ML models, whatever their source
17 to 20Risk-tier all models, review tiers at least annually. A low-complexity score must not reduce the tier of a material model.
29 to 33Independent validation by the RE before and after deployment, after changes, and periodically
45 to 48Third-party models: the RE stays accountable, validates independently regardless of the provider's assurance, and has due diligence, documentation and audit rights in contracts
49 to 51Define the scope of AI and ML models, including foundation and frontier models. Deploy only where the risk can be managed.
52Consider reliance on model outputs and the level of autonomy when tiering AI models
54Set explainability thresholds and apply higher ones to material decisions. Control hallucinations. Test for bias and fairness. Monitor for overfitting and data or concept drift.
55 and 56Red-teaming for customer-facing and generative models. Strict controls over automatic model updates.
57Enhanced documentation for traceability, reproducibility and auditability
58 and 59Deployment safeguards, including controls against prompt injection. Disclose to customers that they are interacting with AI. Offer a route to a human.
60 to 63Human oversight, including override and kill-switch mechanisms, and guarding against automation bias
25Do not use any model that harms consumers. Grievance redressal must cover consumer-facing models.

Note on sources: this entry is based only on the published draft. No consultation submission has been used. Paragraph references are to the draft and may change in the final text.
